We handle prior authorization requests from referring clinics. About 900 a month. Each request
names a patient, a procedure code, a diagnosis code, and carries whatever clinical notes the
referring office attached.

Four nurse reviewers work the queue. For each request they check that the member is eligible on
the date of service, that the procedure is covered under the member's plan, that the diagnosis
and procedure codes are a permitted pairing, and that any prerequisite therapy the policy
requires has been tried and documented.

A large share never needed a person at all. The member's coverage lapsed. The code pairing is
on the auto-approve list and the notes are complete. The same request was already submitted and
approved last week. Our reviewers say the routine ones cost as much attention as the hard ones,
because nothing looks different until you open it.

The judgment is whether the attached documentation actually supports medical necessity as the
policy defines it. The notes are free text written by a busy clinician, the policy language is
specific, and the two rarely use the same words. A reviewer decides whether what is described
meets the criterion. Roughly one request in four.

Anything the reviewer would deny goes to the Medical Director, without exception, because a
denial has to be made by a clinician of equal or greater specialty. Requests marked urgent must
have an answer inside 72 hours. Everything here is PHI, so records stay inside our environment
and no patient identifier may appear in any log we keep.

We once approved a duplicate of a request that had already been denied, because the second
submission used a different referral number. Today the median turnaround is four days.
