Inbound RFPs arrive and somebody has to decide whether we bid. We see roughly 60 a month,
mostly public sector, each between 30 and 200 pages, usually with a mandatory requirements
section and a deadline.

Two people in sales operations read them. For each one they check the submission deadline
against our capacity calendar, whether we hold the certifications the buyer names as mandatory,
whether the contract value is inside the range we serve, whether we are already incumbent or
conflicted, and whether the buyer's required contract terms include anything our legal team has
previously refused.

A lot of them can be answered without reading much. The deadline is inside ten days and we
cannot staff a response that fast. It names a certification we do not hold. The ceiling value
is below our minimum. Those are the majority, and reading two hundred pages to find out is the
part our people hate.

The judgment is whether we can actually meet the mandatory requirements as written, which is
different from whether we could do the work. The requirements are drafted by the buyer, often
reusing language from a previous award, and deciding whether our capability satisfies the words
on the page is a call somebody has to make. About one in four.

Anything above two million dollars in ceiling value requires the VP of Sales to approve a bid
decision. Anything naming a state we are not registered to do business in goes to Legal before
anyone spends time on it. Last year we spent about six weeks of effort on a bid we were
disqualified from because we did not hold a certification that was named on page 140.

We currently take eight days to reach a bid or no-bid decision, and the deadline is usually
thirty.
