Somebody has to audit expense reports before we reimburse them. About 1,200 line items a month
across roughly 300 reports. Each line has an amount, a date, a category the employee chose, a
merchant name, and usually a receipt image.

One person in finance does this alongside other work. For each line they check that a receipt
is attached where the policy requires one, that the amount matches the receipt, that the
category is plausible for that merchant, that the date falls inside the reporting period, and
that the same receipt has not been submitted before.

The overwhelming majority are fine and boring. Under our receipt threshold, so no image is
needed. Amount matches. Merchant obviously a hotel, category hotel. Those should never have
reached a human at all, and today every one of them does.

What needs judgment is business purpose. The policy allows client entertainment and does not
allow personal entertainment, and the difference is usually a sentence the employee wrote in a
hurry, read alongside who else was there and what else happened that week. Our auditor makes
that call perhaps eight times in a hundred lines.

Lines over 75 dollars require a receipt. Any single line over 1,000 dollars needs the employee's
department head to approve it before we pay, regardless of how ordinary it looks. Anything
categorized as a gift goes to the Controller. We have twice reimbursed the same hotel receipt
submitted on two different reports a month apart.

Reports currently take about nine days to clear, and the finance team gets asked about it
constantly.
