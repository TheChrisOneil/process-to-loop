Software features are now cheap to copy. An analyst recently used an AI coding agent to build a working Salesforce competitor in about three hours. Every software company now has to operate as an app factory: build the machine that builds your machines.

Speed alone can be rented. The durable advantage is a factory built for one deeply regulated vertical. a16z's State of Markets 2026 reaches the same conclusion: vertical AI is the standout category, and the moat is context, integrations, security, evaluation, and domain workflow. Regulated markets favor founders who can run factories and certifications.

Healthcare is that vertical. Automating regulated clinical workflows is an active research area, and EverBetter is participating in it. Our research is producing a factory that lets a clinician, a nurse, or a patient create a new workflow in their own space while every safety, security, and HIPAA obligation still holds. The clinician carries the liability. The Federation of State Medical Boards states that "the physician is ultimately responsible for the use of AI and should be held accountable for any harms."

Our research is producing a pipeline. A process owner's description goes in; an approved, executable workflow comes out; every run follows the same path. We drive thinking steps out wherever we can. What remains is highly deterministic, so every decision the workflow makes is exposed and auditable.

We call the engine a deterministic control loop. Code owns every path through the process. A model is called only at declared thinking steps, and each output must pass a gate before the workflow can progress. That makes every use repeatable and every decision auditable.

We build that pipeline in layers.

Process modeling: we apply BPMN and DMN concepts to AI-drafted workflows. Every step is one of five kinds. Rule decisions become decision tables.

Determinism: we refactor the process to remove thinking points. At most one judgment per unit of work. Everything else runs as code.

Accountability: every interaction point is gated. Gates record who decided and on what evidence. A named clinician signs the exact design and knows which liabilities they accept.

Execution: designs compile into formulas the orchestrator runs, with executable gates and an append-only ledger.

Regulatory boundaries: PHI reaches a model step only under a recorded, current business associate agreement. A second model audits every design.

Our research is also generalizing the engine. We have separated the verification rules from the engine. Core rules apply to any process; domain policy lives in a versioned catalog specific to each industry and sub-industry. Healthcare is the first catalog. The same engine has already produced and audited designs for financial processes. One engine plus an industry-specific catalog is a factory of factories: customized workflows across regulated domains of all kinds.

Each layer encodes knowledge of clinical operations and health regulation. That knowledge is the moat.
