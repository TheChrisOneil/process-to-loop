Adoption is solved. Measurement is the problem.

I build automated workflows for healthcare, where every automated decision must survive an auditor. I start every design with one question: what is the Total Value Realized?

TCO is the floor. TVR is the ceiling. The advantage is the spread.

Most business cases count licenses and tokens. That is a quote. Real TCO has seven layers, and tokens are usually the smallest.

TVR is the number that gets misrepresented. 70% of disclosed AI proof points were cost savings; 22% were revenue. Savings are the easy half.

Regulated industries add a term: an outcome you cannot defend to an auditor has negative value.

So I measure cost per completed decision, capture the manual baseline before shipping, and default to deterministic code. The model handles judgment only.

Claude supplies the harness. We own the proprietary context: the prompts and the loop that turn a described process into an auditable workflow.
