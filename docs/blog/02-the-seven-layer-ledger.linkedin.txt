Nine transactions entered a nightly run in my September workshop.

A business rule refused three. Arithmetic resolved two. Four reached a model.

Five of nine cost nothing.

That ratio is the TCO strategy.

Most business cases price the model calls and stop. I account for seven layers: design and build, inference, human review, platform, assurance, quality safeguards, and lifecycle upgrades. Two are one-time capital. Five recur.

In healthcare, platform and assurance carry the HIPAA audit control burden. Omit them and you have priced a workflow nobody can deploy.

Tokens are the small, falling half of OpEx. Review minutes are the expensive, stubborn half. Throughput is bounded by review capacity; exceed it and the workflow builds a queue.

My measured numbers: $0.2557 average to generate a workflow design across 29 runs. About $1.06 to author and independently audit one gate.

The real first-week cost was eight integration defects. Each passed by hand and failed in production. Integration labor belongs on the ledger.

Write the exit threshold down before launch, while everyone is still optimistic.

TCO protects the spread. TVR sets its width.
